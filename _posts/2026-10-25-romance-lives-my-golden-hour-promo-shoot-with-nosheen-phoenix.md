## Who's Nosheen?

Nosheen Phoenix is a British actress, singer-songwriter and model from **London**.

Her [IMDb bio](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm3645738/bio/) says she moved to New York City in **2009** and then to Los Angeles in **2012** to go all in on acting.

Her TV credits on [IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm3645738/) include:

- ***Modern Family*** (Daliya, **2013**)
- ***Grey's Anatomy*** (Whitney, **2013**)
- ***Happy Endings*** (Melissa, **2013**)
- ***Mike & Molly*** (Shyama, **2013**)
- ***The Brink*** on HBO (Nayyara, **4 episodes**, **2015**)

She's also in the feature ***What's Love Got to Do with It?*** (**2022**).

And here's a fun one. Her IMDb bio says she co-wrote Japanese pop star **Kumi Koda's** No. 1 single **"Physical Thing."**

So yes, I was working with a pro. In my post I called her "fabulous." Still true.

## Who's Luke?

Luke Pickerill is a photographer and videographer. His company was **Pickerill Creative**, based in Los Angeles.

His [Vimeo page](https://vimeo.com/user2916646) shows the range: weddings, music videos, and network promos. One of them is a Kids' WB promo for *The Batman* that he lists as a **PROMAX award winner**.

He also wrote a [guest lighting post for Quantum Instruments](https://qtm.com/2012/04/23/okay-i-admit-i-am-a-flasher/) in **April 2012**. In it he admits he's "a flasher." As in, he loves using **flash**.

But he also wrote this: sometimes he sets up all his flashes, sees "incredible available light," and drops them right where he stands.

Look at these photos. You can see he had that kind of day. The sun is doing the work behind us.

## Is chivalry really dead?

I tagged it **#ChivalryIsNotDead**. So let's look at the word.

According to the [Online Etymology Dictionary](https://www.etymonline.com/word/chivalry), "chivalry" shows up in English around **1300**.

It comes from the Old French **chevalerie**. That came from **chevaler**, meaning "knight." And that goes back to the Latin **caballus**: a work horse.

So at first, chivalry literally meant **knights on horses**. Warfare. Cavalry.

By the **late 1300s**, it also meant a code of **honor, valor, generosity and courtly manners**.

That second meaning is the one I mean. Hold the door. Fix the tie. Be kind.

No horse required.
