## The gym in the photo

That pink ring sits inside **Wild Card Boxing Club** on Vine Street in Hollywood.

Trainer **Freddie Roach** opened it in **1995**. The gym's own site still says it: founded by Freddie Roach, established 1995, Hollywood.

He did it on a tiny budget. Roach told Michael Rapaport in *Men's Journal* he had about **$10,000**. He couldn't even afford a real ring. So he framed the first one out of lumber, like a house. He said he had about **$50** left and spent it on flyers.

They made rent the first month.

Here's a fun one. Roach's own mentor, the legendary trainer **Eddie Futch**, told him never to open a gym. Too much work. Roach opened one anyway at age **35**, per the *Los Angeles Times*.

The name? He asked friends to write down ideas, figuring he'd find "a good wild card" in the pile. Then he named it that.

## What came out of that room

The numbers here are wild.

- **42** fighters trained at Wild Card and went on to win world titles (*Los Angeles Times*, 2025).
- Roach has been named boxing trainer of the year **7** times (same source).
- He was inducted into the **International Boxing Hall of Fame** in Canastota, New York, on **June 10, 2012**.
- His most famous fighter, **Manny Pacquiao**, won world titles in **8** weight divisions.

Pacquiao walked in back in **2001**, did mitts with Roach, and they clicked. The gym got so busy that Roach took over the space downstairs as a second gym.

Roach was a fighter first, too. **53** pro fights. Final record: **40-13**.

In **May 2025**, Wild Card turned **30**. The area around the gym was officially named **Freddie Roach Square**.

And it's not just for champs. *Boxing Scene* noted anyone can train there with a day fee or membership. Champions on one bag. Regular folks on the next.

I wrote about the alley *under* this gym in an earlier post: [I Choreographed an Alley Fight Under Wildcard Boxing](/2026/10/16/i-choreographed-an-alley-fight-under-wildcard-boxing/).

## OK, so is boxing the best cardio?

Scientists measure effort in **METs**.

One MET is the energy you burn sitting still. An activity rated **6** METs burns about six times that.

The U.S. government's *Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans* sorts it like this:

- **3.0 to 5.9** METs = moderate
- **6.0 or more** METs = vigorous

Now look at boxing in the **2024 Adult Compendium of Physical Activities**. That's the big research list used to rate exercise.

- Boxing in the ring, general: **12.3** METs
- Simulated boxing round: **9.3** METs
- Sparring: **7.8** METs
- Punching bag, general: **5.8** METs
- Punching bag at **180** punches a minute: **10.8** METs

Compare that with the same list:

- Jogging at your own pace: **7.5** METs
- Running a 10-minute mile: **9.3** METs

So boxing in the ring rates **higher** than a 10-minute-mile run.

Even casual bag work lands near the top of "moderate." Speed it up and you blow past a jog.

## What that means in calories

The Compendium team says one MET is roughly **1 calorie per kilogram of body weight per hour**.

Here's an example for a **154-pound (70 kg)** adult. These are rough estimates, not lab results:

- One hour boxing in the ring (12.3 METs): about **860** calories
- One hour running a 10-minute mile (9.3 METs): about **650** calories
- One hour easy bag work (5.8 METs): about **405** calories

Your number will be different. Body size, skill, and how hard you push all change it. But the ranking holds.

## The weekly math

The federal guidelines say adults should get at least **150 minutes** a week of moderate activity. Or at least **75 minutes** of vigorous activity.

A boxing round is **3 minutes**.

**75 minutes = 25 rounds.**

Spread over a week, that's about **5 rounds a day, 5 days a week**.

Not hours on a treadmill. Just rounds.

The guidelines also say to do muscle-strengthening work at least **2** days a week. So rounds are a great start, not the whole plan.

## Why I keep coming back

Here's my honest take. "Best cardio" is my opinion.

But it's an opinion with numbers behind it.

Boxing makes you move your feet, turn your hips, and throw your hands, over and over, while thinking. You can't zone out. Somebody is moving in front of you.

For stunt work and fight scenes, that matters. Rhythm. Distance. Breath. You learn all three in the ring.

And there's something about working out in a room where **42** world champions trained. You feel it.

So yeah. **#TheBestCardio.** I'm sticking with it.
