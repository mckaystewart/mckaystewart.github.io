## What is Beta Test?

***Beta Test*** is an **88-minute** American sci-fi action thriller. It hit theaters on **July 22, 2016**.

The pitch is wild and simple.

A gaming company builds a first-person shooter that does not stay on the screen. A champion tester named **Max Troy** finds out the "game" is mirroring real events in the city. The on-screen hero, **Orson Creed**, is a real person being remotely controlled. Virtual and real smash together. They have to stop the conspiracy before the missions spill into the streets.

Director **Nicholas Gyeney** (who also wrote and produced) called it a cross between *Die Hard*, *Gamer*, and *The Firm*. Production company: **Mirror Images LTD**. Shot in **Seattle**, Gyeney's hometown.

You can look up the title on [IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt4244162/) (tt4244162). Runtime there: **88 minutes**. Release: **2016**.

## Who I worked next to

**Manu Bennett** plays **Orson Creed** — the man inside the game who will not stay a puppet.

If you know him from elsewhere, you already know the physical presence. *Spartacus*. *The Hobbit* trilogy (Azog). *Arrow* (Deathstroke / Slade Wilson). On this set he was the action center of gravity. My Day **5** note was not polite fluff. He was putting in the work, and he was cool about it.

## Seattle, November 2014

Principal photography ran about **19 days**. It started **November 7, 2014** and wrapped **November 26, 2014**.

That start date is the same calendar day as my Starbucks and waterfront posts.

My earlier post that morning said **Day 5** of rehearsals. So the fight work did not begin when the camera rolled. It started before. That matches what the production talked about in public: a long, choreographed fight sequence that took **weeks** to rehearse. Gyeney wanted a single-shot fight that would smash the old record (he pointed at the famous *Oldboy* hallway take). The finished film is known for a fight stretch of more than **6 minutes** in one continuous shot.

Seattle was not a random backdrop. Gyeney picked his hometown on purpose. He wanted more action work in a local scene that usually leans drama. Locations included spots around the city — the climactic fight was even filmed at **Seattle City Hall**, per *The Seattle Times*.

Budget reports put the film under **$2 million**. For that money they still got national theatrical play in **2016** (Seattle premiere, then cities including New York, Los Angeles, and San Francisco).

## Coffee, creed, and an Inosanto shirt

The Starbucks photo is one of my favorites from that week.

Manu's arm over my shoulder. Me in a black **Inosanto Academy, Los Angeles** shirt. Industrial lights. The classic Starbucks seal on the wall behind us. Cool filter. Zero Hollywood stiffness.

That shirt is not random merch. I train martial arts hard — Wushu, Jeet Kune Do, Muay Thai, Tae Kwon Do, Kali, Jiu-Jitsu — and the Inosanto Academy is part of that world. Showing up to a Manu Bennett action rehearsal in that shirt felt right.

Hashtag on the post: **#inosantoreps**. Representation and training in one frame.

The second shot is quieter. Me on a railing above the old waterfront highway, looking out at Elliott Bay and the Great Wheel. Sepia. Still. After days of fight rehearsal, you take the breath when you get it.

## Why this stretch of 2014 still hits

**2014** stacked jobs.

- **April:** stunt doubling **Scott Wolf** on TNT's *Perception* ([that post](/2026/10/09/holy-moly-i-doubled-scott-wolf-on-perception/))
- **August:** wrapped *For Blood* as Dead Card Danny ([that post](/2026/10/10/holy-moly-i-played-dead-card-danny-in-for-blood/))
- **November:** Seattle with Manu Bennett on *Beta Test*
- Later that same November: I booked *Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.* (next up in this series)

Stunt day. Fantasy Western short. Sci-fi feature with a Spartacus-level action lead. Then Marvel. That is the working actor / stunt life. You fly north. You rehearse until your body knows the hit. You grab a coffee photo because the moment is real. Then you go home and take the next call.

*Beta Test* is not a quiet indie drama. It is loud on purpose. Controllers. Implants. Street fights. A long take that makes your shoulders ache just watching it.

I got to stand next to the guy carrying that fight — and get credited as **Richter** when the movie landed.
