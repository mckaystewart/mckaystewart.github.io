**February 6, 2021.** I landed my first ollie air-out of the bowl at Venice Beach Skatepark.

I was not a kid. I was a working actor and stuntman. And on a skateboard, I was a total beginner.

Government shutdowns gave me the chance. When work stopped, the parks were still there. Venice, La Puente, Santa Clarita — that's where I put the free time, learning to skate one session at a time.

I posted this photo and wrote: "First ollie air-out of the bowl, whoop!"

![McKay Stewart in the air on a skateboard above the bowl at Venice Beach Skatepark, palm trees and blue sky behind him](/assets/images/2026-10-26/first-ollie-air-venice-2021.jpg)
*My first ollie air-out of the bowl. From my [X post on February 6, 2021](https://x.com/mckaystewart/status/1358094237468774400).*

I thanked my friends at the park for the tips. "Especially the kids," I wrote. "Some of these kids are INSANE!"

That photo kicked off a whole year of skating. Here's how it went, one small win at a time.

## The park

Venice Beach Skatepark sits right on the sand. You can see the ocean while you skate.

It opened on **October 3, 2009**, according to the [LA Parks blog](http://laparks.blogspot.com/2009/10/). Local skaters pushed for it for about **10 years** first.

The park covers **16,000 square feet**. It cost **$3.4 million**, per the [Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2009-oct-04-me-venice-skate-park4-story.html).

It has **4 main parts**:

- A deep pool, up to **9 feet**
- A street course with steps and rails
- A **snake run**, a winding path you carve from bank to bank
- A flow bowl

Venice is also Dogtown. That's where the Z-Boys helped reinvent skating in the **1970s**. The park is officially named for one of them, Dennis "Polar Bear" Agnew, as [Surfer Today](https://www.surfertoday.com/skateboarding/the-story-of-venice-beach-skatepark) explains.

So yes. I was a beginner learning on legendary concrete.

## Wait, what's an ollie?

Quick lesson, since I'll use a lot of skate words.

An **ollie** is when you and the board jump into the air with no hands. You pop the tail down, jump, and slide your front foot forward. That's how [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ollie_(skateboarding)) puts it.

It goes back to **1978**. A skater named Alan "Ollie" Gelfand did it in bowls and pools. Then Rodney Mullen took it to flat ground in **1982**.

An **air-out** means leaving the bowl and flying out over the edge. For me, that was a big deal.

## Baby steps, on purpose

On **April 7, 2021**, I posted a clip and called myself a "skate park n00b." I'd been skating Venice, La Puente and Santa Clarita.

My summary? "Mostly not falling."

I thanked Holland Diaz for the videos and tips. I thanked "all my homies and home girls at Venice Skate Park" too.

That's the honest truth of learning anything. You fall. You get back up. You try again.

I landed my first **ollie fakie**. "Fakie" means rolling backward, while you stand in your normal stance.

## The grind season

In the fall, I moved on to **grinds**. A grind is sliding along an edge on your trucks, the metal parts that hold the wheels.

At a skatepark, that edge is usually the **coping**, the metal pipe along the top of a bowl.

**November 19:** my first **frontside grind**. Frontside means you face the wall. I thanked "Sifu" Kyle Jetter for the tutorial.

## First backside grind

**December 23, 2021.** The Cove Skatepark in Santa Monica, at night.

I landed my first **backside grind**. Backside means your back faces the wall. It's harder to see where you're going.

How long did it take? "Ten tries and much pain later," I wrote.

I was also going higher on the **oververt**, a wall that tips past straight up. "It's much higher than it looks!" I said. "Hitting that thing's like a roller coaster."

## Why a stuntman learns to skate

Stunt work is about body control. Balance. Falling safely. Staying calm when things move fast.

Skating trains all of that. Every session is practice at being scared and doing it anyway. My hashtag for it was **#overcomefear**.

And it keeps you humble. In February I was learning from kids. By December I was still posting **#babysteps**.

That's the point. Small wins add up. **One ollie** turned into a year of firsts.

The story kept going in 2022. That's a post for another day.
