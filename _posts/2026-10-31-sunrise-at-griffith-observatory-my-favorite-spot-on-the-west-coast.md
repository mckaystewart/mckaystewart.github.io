Here's why this spot is so special. And why it's free for you, too.

## One man's wild gift

The story starts with a guy named **Griffith J. Griffith**.

He was born in Wales in **1850**. He came to America as a teen. He got rich in Mexican silver mines, then in Southern California real estate.

In **1882** he bought what was left of an old Spanish ranch called **Rancho Los Felis**.

Then he gave a big chunk of it away.

On **December 16, 1896**, he donated **3,015 acres** to the City of Los Angeles. That land became **Griffith Park**.

He said it should be "a place of rest and relaxation for the masses." A park for "the plain people."

Today the Observatory calls Griffith Park the **largest urban-wilderness municipal park in the United States**.

## The telescope that changed his mind

Griffith once looked through the **60-inch telescope** at Mount Wilson. At the time, it was the biggest in the world.

It blew his mind. He reportedly said: "If all mankind could look through that telescope, it would change the world!"

So on **December 12, 1912**, he offered the city **$100,000** to build a public observatory on Mount Hollywood. His plan had **three** big ideas:

- A telescope anyone could look through for **free**
- A Hall of Science with exhibits
- A theater for science films

Politics got in the way. The project stalled for years.

Griffith knew he might not live to see it. In **1916** he wrote his plan into his will. He died on **July 6, 1919**.

## Built in the Great Depression

The dream finally moved in **1930**. Top scientists joined the team, including **George Ellery Hale**, the man behind the giant telescopes at Mount Wilson and Palomar.

Groundbreaking came on **June 20, 1933**.

Two things shaped the building:

**1. An earthquake.** A major quake hit Long Beach in **March 1933**. So the architects dropped the planned terra cotta outside. They went with thicker, stronger concrete walls instead.

**2. The Depression.** Prices were low. So the builders could afford the finest materials of the day.

The old movie-theater idea changed too. The planetarium was invented in **1923**, after Griffith died. His family agreed it fit his vision even better. Griffith's planetarium became the **third** in the United States.

And the public telescope? A **12-inch Zeiss** refractor. The planners judged it the best of its kind you could buy.

The Observatory opened on **May 14, 1935**.

## That tall white tower

See the pointy monument in my photo? That's the **Astronomers Monument**.

It was made during the Great Depression by a federal art program called the **Public Works of Art Project**. That program put out-of-work artists back to work.

**Six** artists each sculpted **one** famous astronomer:

- **Hipparchus** (about 125 B.C.)
- **Nicolaus Copernicus**
- **Galileo Galilei**
- **Johannes Kepler**
- **Isaac Newton**
- **William Herschel**

Fun fact: one of those artists, **George Stanley**, also created the famous **Oscar** statuette.

The monument was finished in **1934**. Its only "signature" is "PWAP 1934." It stands about **37.5 feet** tall with the armillary sphere on top.

Albert Einstein almost made the cut. The planners passed because he was still alive.

## The view

This is why I go.

From the terrace you see the whole city. Downtown towers. Neighborhoods rolling out to the coast. On a clear day you can see for miles.

## A big makeover

The Observatory closed for a huge renovation on **January 6, 2002**. It reopened on **November 3, 2006**.

The price tag: **$93 million**.

From **1935 to 2002**, it had already welcomed about **70 million** visitors.

## Want to go?

Good news. Griffith's big idea still holds. **Admission is free.**

A few tips from the Observatory itself:

- It's **closed Mondays**.
- Weekends it opens at **10 a.m.** Tuesday to Friday it opens at **noon**.
- The grounds and roads open at **5 a.m.** That's how I catch those early mornings.
- Parking right by the building costs money. There's **free parking** downhill.
- No drones. And sadly for me, **no skateboards** on the grounds.

Go early. Bring water. Make a silly face at the city.

One of the nicest spots on the west coast. I stand by it.
