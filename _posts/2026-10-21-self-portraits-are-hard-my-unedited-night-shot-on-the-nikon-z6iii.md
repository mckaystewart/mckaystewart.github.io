## Why shooting yourself is so hard

When I shoot someone else, I'm behind the camera. I can see the frame. I can see their face. I can move a light an inch and check it again.

When I shoot myself, I can't see any of that.

So the job turns into a loop:

1. Set the camera on a tripod.
2. Set the lights on stands.
3. Guess where to stand.
4. Walk in. Hold still. Take the shot.
5. Walk back. Check it. Fix one thing.
6. Do it again.

And again. And again.

Focus is the hardest part. At night, with a lens like this, the zone that's sharp can be razor thin. Lean back a little and your eyes go soft.

That's why I said it was hard. I wasn't kidding.

## The gear

I tagged the post with **#nikon**, **#z6iii** and **#85mm**. That's the camera and the kind of lens.

The **Nikon Z6III** is pretty new. Nikon announced it on **June 17, 2024**. A few facts from [Nikon's own press release](https://www.nikonusa.com/press-room/nikon-z6iii):

- **24.5** megapixels.
- Nikon called it the **world's first** camera with a "partially-stacked" CMOS sensor. In plain words, that's a sensor built to read out super fast.
- Nikon says its autofocus can work in light as dark as **-10 EV**. That's very dark. Think nighttime sports.

That last one matters for a night portrait. The camera has to find my eyes in the dark.

The **85mm** focal length is a classic for portraits. When Nikon launched its [NIKKOR Z 85mm f/1.2 S](https://www.nikonusa.com/press-room/85mm-26mm) on **February 6, 2023**, it said this length gives a "flattering, compressing" look that "dissolves the background" into bokeh.

## So what's bokeh?

**Bokeh** is the soft blur behind the subject. Out-of-focus lights turn into round balls of color.

Look at the photo again. Every glowing circle back there is a real light on the street. A sign. A car. A window. The lens just melts them.

You get more of that when:

- the lens is long, like **85mm**,
- the aperture is wide open,
- and the background is far behind you.

The blur pushes your eye right to the face. That's the whole point.

## Two lights, no fixes

I said **two lights**. The street gave me the background glow. My lights handled my face.

And I said **unedited, no filters**. So what you see is what the camera caught. No smoothing. No color tricks. No fake blur added later.

That's a test I keep giving myself and this camera.

## Why an actor shoots his own portraits

I'm an actor and a stuntman. I tagged the post that way too, along with **#agentsofshield** and **#csi**.

Actors need photos all the time. Headshots. Looks. Casting profiles. If you can light and shoot yourself, you can make new ones any night you want.

It also makes me better on the other side of the camera. When I know what a lens does, I know where to stand on a set. I know why the camera team wants me on my mark.
