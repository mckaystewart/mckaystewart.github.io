## The movie: Mad Cowgirl

**March 13, 2021.** I posted three photos and wrote: "Another great memory, working with some crazy guys and gals on an even crazier movie."

The movie was ***Mad Cowgirl***. It's an indie film written and directed by **Gregory Hatanaka**.

My jobs on it:

- **Stunt coordinator.** I planned and ran the stunts.
- **Actor.** I played a character named **Alan Razor**.

Both credits are on the film's [cast and crew list](https://www.metacritic.com/movie/mad-cowgirl/credits/).

## What's it about?

The main character is **Therese**, played by **Sarah Lassez**. She's a meat inspector with a brain disorder.

As her mind slips, she starts seeing the men in her life as the **Ten Tigers of Canton**. That's a famous group from Chinese martial arts legend. And in her head, she has to fight them.

So yes. Lots of swords. Lots of fights. That's where my team came in.

The cast also had **Walter Koenig**, Chekov from the original ***Star Trek***. Plus **James Duval** and **Devon Odessa**.

*Staffs, swords and fake blood. Our fight team posing on set. From my [X post on March 13, 2021](https://x.com/mckaystewart/status/1370827455557083136).*

## Where it played

***Mad Cowgirl*** was picked for **SF IndieFest** and the **Silver Lake Film Festival**. Then it had a limited run in cities like **New York** and **Seattle**.

It came out in **2006**. The DVD followed in **December 2006**.

That's [per its Wikipedia page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mad_Cowgirl), which cites *Variety* and *The Seattle Times*.

## The crew I tagged

This is the fun part. Look who was on my stunt team.

**Sam Hargrave** was my **assistant stunt coordinator**.

Today he's director **Sam Hargrave**. He directed ***Extraction*** (**2020**) and ***Extraction 2*** (**2023**) for Netflix.

Before that, he was stunt coordinator on Marvel's ***Captain America: Civil War***, ***Avengers: Infinity War*** and ***Avengers: Endgame***. He was also second unit director on season **2** of ***The Mandalorian***.

And ***Mad Cowgirl*** sits at the top of the stunt coordinator credits on [his Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Hargrave). Pretty cool to see where it started.

I also tagged **Fernando Chien**. He's an actor and stunt performer. You've seen him in ***Fast Five***, ***Warrior*** and ***Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings***. He was also stunt coordinator on ***The Accountant*** ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Chien)).

**Kym Stys** did stunts on the film and played the **Beach Hostage**.

And I gave a shoutout to **Vic Chao**, who played **Charlie**. These days, gamers know his voice. He's **Kenshi** in ***Mortal Kombat 1*** ([source](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt26862060/characters/nm0152059)).

## Why a stuntman dances

Stunts and dance have a lot in common.

You learn a move. You hit a mark. You do it again. And again. Every take has to match.

A fight scene is really just a dance where people pretend to get hurt. Same counts. Same timing. Same trust in your partner.

So when friends ask me to dance? I say yes.

## Quick facts for new fans

**Naruto** started as a manga by **Masashi Kishimoto**. It ran from **1999** to **2014**. Then came the anime, including ***Naruto Shippuden***, which ran **500** episodes ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naruto)).

***Back to the Future*** came out in **1985**. Robert Zemeckis directed it. Michael J. Fox starred as Marty McFly ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_the_Future)).

Two of my all-time favorites. In one photo. 'Nuff said.

## What these three have in common

A stunt team. A dance crew. A convention floor.

All three are about people. Friends who show up, work hard, and have fun doing it.

In 2021, I took the slow months to look back and say thanks.

And if you're just starting out? Remember this. The people next to you on a tiny indie film might be directing Netflix movies one day.

Be good to your crew.
