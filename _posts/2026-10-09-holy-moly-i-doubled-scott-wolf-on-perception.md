Holy moly. April 2014. Brand-new X account. Old one got hacked, so I started fresh.

Day one I yelled about a *West Side Swordy* trailer (I already wrote a whole post about that film — [go read it](/2026/10/08/holy-moly-west-side-swordy/) if you missed it).

**Two weeks later** I posted a set photo and this line:

> "worked on #perception stunt doubling actor #scottwolf from #BreakingBad and #partyoffive. good times!"

That was me. On TNT. Doubling **Scott Wolf**.

Twelve years later, I'm expanding that tiny post into the full story — with the photo, the credits, and the proof.

## So what was Perception?

*Perception* was a TNT crime drama that ran **2012 to 2015**.

**Eric McCormack** (Will from *Will & Grace*) played Dr. Daniel Pierce — a neuroscience professor the FBI calls when a case gets weird. **Rachael Leigh Cook** played agent Kate Moretti, his former student. The show's hook was Pierce's brain: he sees patterns other people miss.

Then **Scott Wolf** joined as **Donnie Ryan**.

If you grew up in the '90s, you already know Scott as Bailey Salinger from *Party of Five*. On *Perception*, Donnie was Kate's complicated ex — a fast-rising Assistant U.S. Attorney who transferred back to Chicago. *The Hollywood Reporter* called it a **major recurring role** when he signed on for season two in 2013. Wikipedia lists him on the show across **2013–2015**.

My job: be the body double when Donnie needs a stunt. Take the hit. Drive the car. Stay in frame so the star stays safe.

**Stunt doubling. Stunt driving. The good stuff.**

## The photo

Look at it. Orange prison jumpsuits. Outdoor yard. Gym gear in the background. Whole group grinning like we just wrapped something dumb and fun.

That's a real behind-the-scenes day. Not a press kit. Not a posed promo. Just the crew and cast-adjacent folks between takes, phones out, capturing the moment.

I slapped hashtags on it and hit post. That was the whole "article" in 2014. Two sentences and a pic.

## See it in motion

Clips from *Perception* made it into my action and stunt demo reel, along with *Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.* and *Eagle Eye*. [Watch the reel on X](https://x.com/mckaystewart/status/1377767989924093954).

## The credit (on the record)

Here's the part you can look up without trusting my memory:

- **Metacritic** lists me on *Perception* as **“stunt double: Scott Wolf / stunt driver”** for **2 episodes in 2014**.
- **IMDb** credits me as a **stunt driver** on the season 3 episode **“Dirty”** ([tt3765302](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt3765302/fullcredits/)).

**“Dirty”** aired **August 19, 2014** on TNT. Runtime about 45 minutes. Directed by **LeVar Burton**. Short version of the plot: Donnie lands in a compromising situation, calls Pierce for help, and the evidence starts stacking against him while Pierce and Moretti work the case.

So the timeline goes:

1. **April 2014** — I'm on set, post the photo.
2. **August 2014** — the episode hits TV with my name in the stunt credits.

That's how TV works. You sweat in spring. It airs in summer.

## Quick honesty about that hashtag

My caption said Scott Wolf was “from #BreakingBad and #partyoffive.”

**Party of Five:** correct. He played Bailey for years.

**Breaking Bad:** that was me mixing shows in a hashtag rush. Scott Wolf was not on *Breaking Bad*. (He did later show up on *The Night Shift*, which shares some cast overlap with other crime shows — but that's not the same thing.)

I'm leaving the original post as-is on X. History is history. But on this blog I correct the record: the verified work is *Perception*, Scott Wolf, stunt double / stunt driver.

## Why a 12-year-old set photo still hits

Because that account was brand new, and that photo was me saying: **I'm in this world.**

Not dreaming about it. Not posting gym clips and hoping. On a network set. Doubling a name people recognize. Building a reel one call sheet at a time.

Stunt work is strange and great. You train so someone else's face can sell the story. When it works, nobody in the audience should notice you. When the credits roll, your name is there anyway.

*Perception* was not my whole career. It was an early brick. A real one. Network TV. Searchable credits. A photo I can still point at and say: I was there.

Holy moly. Not a bad second photo-post for a fresh account.

If you're a young performer reading this: post the work. Keep the pictures. Years later you'll want the proof.
